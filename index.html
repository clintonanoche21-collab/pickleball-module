Module 1 · Pickleball History

By the end, you can: Explain who created pickleball, when and where, and analyze three turning points in its growth.

Read

  • 1965: On Bainbridge Island, Washington, Joel Pritchard, Bill Bell and Barney McCallum invented the game to entertain bored families. They used a badminton court, a Wiffle-type ball and improvised paddles (first ping-pong paddles, then plywood).
  • The name: two stories exist. One credits Pritchard's dog, Pickles; the other compares the game to a "pickle boat," a crew made of leftover rowers. The story of leftovers fits the game itself.
  • 1967: first permanent court. 1972: Pickle-Ball, Inc. formed. 1976: first known tournament, in Tukwila, Washington.
  • 1984: the USA Pickleball Association was created and the first official rulebook was published. The first composite paddle (Arlen Paranto) also appeared.
  • 1990: played in all 50 US states. 2019: professional tours (PPA and APP) launched. 2021: Major League Pickleball. 2022: Washington named pickleball its official state sport.

Module 2 · Pickleball Court and Equipment

By the end, you can: Identify the parts of the court, apply its measurements, and justify equipment choices.

Read

  • Court: 20 ft wide by 44 ft long, the same size as a doubles badminton court. Singles and doubles use the same court.
  • Net: 36 in at the sidelines, 34 in at the center.
  • Kitchen (non-volley zone): the 7-ft area on each side of the net. The kitchen line is part of the zone.
  • Lines: baseline, sideline, centerline, kitchen line. All are 2 in wide, and a ball touching a line is in (except a serve landing on the kitchen line).
  • Paddle: solid, no strings, made of composite, graphite or wood. Beginners usually choose a mid-weight composite paddle for control.
  • Ball: perforated plastic. Outdoor balls are harder with smaller holes; indoor balls are softer with larger holes. Wear court shoes with lateral support.

Module 3 · Pickleball Rules and Gameplay

By the end, you can: Apply the serve, two-bounce, kitchen and scoring rules to game situations.

Read

  • Serve: underhand, contact below the waist, diagonally cross-court, one attempt only. It must clear the kitchen and its line.
  • Two-bounce rule: the serve must bounce, then the return must bounce, before anyone may volley.
  • Kitchen rule: no volleying while standing in the kitchen or on its line, including if your momentum carries you in. You may enter to hit a ball that has bounced.
  • Scoring: only the serving side scores. Games go to 11, win by 2. In doubles, call three numbers: your score, their score, server number (1 or 2).
  • Faults: ball out of bounds, into the net, a double bounce on your side, or a kitchen volley.

Module 4 · Pickleball Competition

By the end, you can: Compare competition formats and evaluate the factors behind pickleball's growth.

Read

  • Governing bodies: USA Pickleball sets the official rulebook; amateur and pro events build on it.
  • Formats: singles, doubles and mixed doubles. Recreational games go to 11; tournaments may use 15 or 21, always win by 2.
  • Pro tours: the PPA Tour and Major League Pickleball (a team format, begun in 2021). Both now sit under one parent company, Pickleball Inc.
  • Season: the PPA Tour moved from a calendar year to a fall-through-spring season. Its 2025-26 Finals were held in May at San Clemente, California.
  • Championship Sunday: PPA finals are best-of-five games, where wins and comebacks happen.
  • Growth: the Sports & Fitness Industry Association named it America's fastest-growing sport for three years running (2023 report).
